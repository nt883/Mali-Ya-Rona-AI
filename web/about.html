WHY THIS PROJECT EXISTS

Public money should end in public value.

Somewhere between a budget, a tender, a supplier, a payment and a completed project, the lived experience of a community can disappear from the record.

Mali Ya Rona was created to reconnect the money with the evidence of what people actually received.

Understand the problem ↓
THE GAP

A project can be complete on paper while the problem remains outside someone's door.

Public accountability cannot end when a transaction is recorded. Citizens experience government through water coming from a tap, a road they can travel safely, working streetlights, functioning clinics, completed classrooms and infrastructure that actually works.

Mali Ya Rona asks three simple questions:

01 What was authorised?

What project, tender, budget or service was officially approved?

02 What was paid?

What expenditure, supplier transaction or transfer was recorded?

03 What was delivered?

What evidence shows that the promised service or infrastructure actually reached the community?

BEHIND EVERY RECORD IS A PERSON

Numbers do not carry buckets of water. People do.

An accounting discrepancy can sound technical until it becomes a grandmother buying water from her social grant, a learner starting the day without running water at home, a clinic worker carrying water manually, or a family waiting for infrastructure that was supposed to change daily life.

The purpose of accountability technology is not to make louder accusations. It is to make the evidence easier to follow.

OUR RESPONSE

Follow the money. Follow the evidence. Keep the human in the loop.

The monitoring engine reads structured financial, procurement and project records and connects them into a traceable project branch.

Compare expenditure against comparable prices
Reconcile reported sent and received amounts
Detect missing evidence, overdue work and conflicting records
Connect official status with community observations
Explain why something was flagged using the underlying values
EVIDENCE BEFORE CONCLUSIONS

A review flag is not an accusation.

A high price can have a legitimate explanation. A late project may have an authorised extension. A community report may be mistaken. A transfer difference may be caused by timing, reversals or an authorised alternative payment.

Mali Ya Rona surfaces the discrepancy and the evidence. A human reviewer determines what it means.
